Far from any stage, in a living room, Bhimrao sings for Dr. Prakash Amte and Dr. Mandakini Amte — the doctor couple who have given their lives to serving the Madia Gond people at Hemalkasa. Two lifetimes of service, one ghazal.

The Story

From a village in Amravati to every Marathi heartअमरावतीच्या मातीतून मराठी मनाच्या मैफलीपर्यंत

I · The beginning

Born on 30 March 1951 in Ashtagaon, Amravati district, in the heart of Vidarbha, Bhimrao Panchale began learning classical music at the age of nine.

He trained for eight years before he ever sang in public — the ragas first, the words later. He learned Urdu so that he could understand the ghazal from the inside, not merely sing it. In those early years he performed sugam sangeet across Maharashtra, slowly finding his way toward the form that would define his life.

II · Rajkamal Chowk

At Rajkamal Chowk in Amravati, a young listener sat in a corner and heard Suresh Bhat — the poet who would become the Ghazal Samrat of Marathi.

That meeting changed everything. Bhat was writing ghazals in Marathi at a time when many believed the form belonged only to Urdu. He saw in the young singer the voice his words needed. “I give the words,” Bhat told him, “you turn them into gold.”

In 1972, Bhimrao presented his first concert of Marathi ghazals. It was the start of a fifty-year mission.

III · The mehfil

After a decade of touring, he moved to Mumbai — the city he credits with giving him his platform.

From there his mehfils travelled across India and to 17–18 countries, including the United States. More than two thousand evenings later, his approach has not changed: meaning before ornament, feeling before technique. He sings the poet’s thought so clearly that audiences hear the ghazal for the first time, every time.

He meant to retire at sixty. The audience would not let him.

IV · In his own words

In conversation with BBC News Marathi, he looks back on a life measured in couplets — and still answers in verse.

Five decades and two thousand mehfils on, the voice is unhurried and the humility intact. Turn the sound on to hear him.

Fifty years of ghazal. Seventy-five of life.गझलची पन्नाशी, आयुष्याची पंचाहत्तरी

Weighed not in gold, but in books.

To mark fifty years of Marathi ghazal and his seventy-fifth birthday, friends, admirers and dignitaries gathered to honour him. At the heart of the evening was a granthtula: Bhimrao on one pan of a great scale, bundles of books on the other. For a singer who has spent his life giving voice to poets, no tribute could fit better.

Then came the garlands, the citation, the speeches — and finally, as always, the music.

Legacy

Planting the next gardenमी इथे हे अमृताचे रोपटे रुजवून गेलो

A ghazal lives only if it is passed on. Bhimrao has spent as much of his life teaching as singing.

Through his Gazal Sagar Pratishthan he has championed the Akhil Bharatiya Marathi Gazal Sammelan, gathering poets and singers from across the state. He set up a reading room in Ashtgaon named after Suresh Bhat, and has conducted 108 ghazal workshops for young poets and singers — always urging them to find their own voice rather than imitate his.

His daughter, Dr. Bhagyashree Panchale, now shares his stage — carrying the ghazal to a new generation.
